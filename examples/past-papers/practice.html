Original practice questions · Cosine rule · Higher

1. Same method, new numbers

Two sides of a triangle are 5 cm and 8 cm. The angle between them is \(60^\circ\). Find the length of the opposite side.

Show worked answer

Use the cosine rule with the included angle:

\[a^2=5^2+8^2-2(5)(8)\cos60^\circ=49\]

Take the positive square root: \(a=7\text{ cm}\).

2. Apply it in a new context

Two straight paths leave the same point at an angle of \(60^\circ\). One is 8 m long and the other is 11 m long. How far apart are their endpoints? Give your answer to 3 significant figures.

Show worked answer

The two paths form two sides of a triangle:

\[d^2=8^2+11^2-2(8)(11)\cos60^\circ=97\]

So \(d=\sqrt{97}=9.85\text{ m}\), to 3 significant figures.

3. Connect two ideas

A triangle has sides 7 cm, 9 cm and \(\sqrt{67}\) cm. Find the angle between the 7 cm and 9 cm sides, then find the area of the triangle to 3 significant figures.

Show worked answer

Rearrange the cosine rule to find the included angle:

\[\cos A=\frac{7^2+9^2-(\sqrt{67})^2}{2(7)(9)}=\frac12\]

Hence \(A=60^\circ\). Now use the area formula:

\[\text{Area}=\tfrac12(7)(9)\sin60^\circ=27.3\text{ cm}^2\]
